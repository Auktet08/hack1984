## 1. Why

- The model never writes its own quote. It picks a clause number and code copies the clause (citation validity 1.0
  by construction). So **every model quote is a clause, and the trimmer decides its span F1.**
- Today's numbers on the 46 dev free-text gold aspects (E018): whole sentence **0.395**, best clause cut to 14 words
  **0.673**. Rules L3's cue-only spans score 0.731 on the aspects they match.
- Span F1 is one of the 7 averaged metrics, so composite moves by about 1/7 of the span F1 change. Expected gain:
  **about +0.01 composite** (0.673 → about 0.75), at most about +0.02. That is below dev noise for composite, but
  span F1 on the 46 quotes can be measured directly, so the step is checkable.
- The same function shortens quotes in the demo's cards and on transcripts.

## 2. How the scorer judges a quote

`drug track/scoring/score.py:51` (`span_f1`):
- lowercase, split on whitespace, compare as bags of words (token F1);
- **punctuation stays attached** ("survival." ≠ "survival"), so strip trailing punctuation;
- averaged **only over matched aspects** (`score.py:96`).

Precision matters as much as recall: a 14-word quote around a 7-word gold quote scores 0.67 even though it contains
the whole answer.

## 3. What gold quotes look like (dev, 46 free-text aspects)

- **Length:** median 8 words, middle half 6–10, max 14. **None ends in punctuation.**
- **Kept:** the claim, its outcome words and the comparator ("…OS improvement **over osimertinib**",
  "…survival **versus osimertinib**").
- **Cut, most often first:**
  1. **Lead-ins** before the claim: "This is the first and only study to show", "These findings mark the first study
     to demonstrate", "We report a (rare) case of", "Additionally, we", "However,", section labels ("CONCLUSION:",
     "METHODS:").
  2. **Setting and population tails:** "…over placebo ~~in the China cohort~~", "…OS benefit ~~at eight years
     compared to placebo~~", "…EGFR TKIs ~~for advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC~~", "…dosing regimen ~~at their next
     scheduled dose~~".
  3. **Long subjects, sometimes** ("~~Osimertinib after definitive CRT~~ demonstrated PFS benefit"). Short subjects
     stay ("Amivantamab plus lazertinib significantly improved…"). **Inconsistent, so out of scope.**

## 4. Design

`drug track/src/inference/clauses.py`:
- `split(text) -> [{id, start, end, text}]`: as specified in the handoff, stage 4.
- `trim(text, start, end, n=14) -> (start, end)`: returns **offsets into the item text**, never a new string. The
  quote is always `item_text[start:end]`.

`trim` steps, in order:
1. **Lead-in strip.** Remove one leading match from a short fixed list, case-insensitive, at the start of the clause:
   section labels (`^[A-Z ]{3,}:`), discourse openers (however, additionally, moreover, overall, in conclusion,
   in summary, notably), report openers (we report a (rare)? case of, we (found|identified|observed|showed)( that)?,
   (results|data|findings) (showed|suggest|indicate)( that)?, this is the first( and only)? study to (show|demonstrate),
   these findings mark the first study to demonstrate).
2. **Tail strip.** Cut from the first match of a short fixed list of setting/population openers to the end:
   ` in patients`, ` in (the|a|this) [^,]{0,40}(cohort|setting|population|subgroup)`, ` for (advanced|patients|those)`,
   ` at (week|their|\d)`, ` compared (to|with) placebo`. **Never cut at** ` over ` or ` versus `/` vs`.
3. **Cap.** If still over `n` words, keep the `n`-word window with the most aspect cue words (reuse the cue lists in
   `src/rules/predict.py`); ties go to the earliest window.
4. **Strip** trailing punctuation and spaces.
5. **Guards.** If steps 1–3 leave fewer than 3 words, or drop every cue word the clause had, return the step-3-only
   result (plain cap). Never return an empty span.

**Write the lists from the guide's rule ("the SHORTEST exact phrase") and the general patterns in §3, once, before
looking at per-item results.** Don't add a phrase because one dev item needs it. L2 and L3 were fitted to dev the
same way and are probably lower on test (E023).

## 5. Measure (one run, no sweep)

Script: `drug track/src/eval/measure_trim.py` (standard library only).

**A. Dev gold (the yardstick).** For each of the 46 free-text gold aspects (sources other than ctgov and fda_event):
pick the clause with the highest span F1 against gold (oracle selection, as in E018), then report mean span F1 for:

| Variant | Expected |
|---|---|
| whole sentence | 0.395 (E018) |
| best clause, untrimmed | — |
| best clause, plain 14-word cap | 0.673 (E018), the bar |
| best clause + `trim` (n = 14) | target ≥ 0.72 |
| best clause + `trim` (n = 8) | tried **once**, per KTD8 |

**B. Generalisation check (not dev).** On the 1,064 exact teacher quotes in
`drug track/silver/unlabeled_gemini.checked.jsonl`: for each, take the clause that contains most of its words, trim
it, and report the share of teacher-quote words kept, before and after trimming. Teacher quotes run long, so this is
not a score. It catches lists that cut claim words on text the rules were never fitted to. Also print 20 random
before/after pairs from unlabeled items and read them.

**Keep `trim` if:** A beats the plain cap by **≥ 0.03** span F1 **and** B's kept-word share drops by **≤ 10 points**.
Otherwise ship the plain 14-word cap and log why.

**Log it.** `log_exp.py` needs a score.py-shaped JSON. Write `out/dev/score_trim.json` as
`{"all": {"composite": null, "signal_macro_f1": null, "actionable_f1": null, "aspect_f1": null,
"polarity_qwk": null, "span_f1": <A, trim n=14>}}` and log an `info` row whose note gives every variant in A and the
B numbers.

## 7. Tests (`drug track/tests/test_clauses.py`, `unittest`)

Use made-up sentences, not dev text:
- `trim` always returns offsets with `text[start:end]` a substring and no trailing punctuation.
- "This is the first study to show a significant OS improvement over drug X." → starts at "a significant", keeps
  "over drug X".
- "Drug X improved PFS in patients with advanced disease" → ends at "PFS".
- A clause that would trim to under 3 words returns the plain cap instead.
- A 30-word clause returns at most 14 words, including the cue word.
- An empty clause returns an empty span without raising.

## 9. Rules

- Dev, unlabeled and the guide only. **Never open `test_inputs*.jsonl`.**
- One measurement, plus the single n = 8 try. No tuning of lists or thresholds after seeing per-item scores.
- Don't ask the model to trim. A 2B copies quotes exactly only 64–73% of the time (report C), and it costs output
  tokens.
