Rules alone score 0.843 on dev. Every point that's left sits in four places: aspects the rules miss, the shape of spans, the actionable call, and signal type. Each is worth about 1 to 3 points on test, and none of them shows up reliably on 60 dev items. So the first job is a scoreboard that can see a 1-point change. Then point the model at clauses, not sentences, and let it add an aspect whenever it's right more often than not.

Codebase Context

Where we are

It is Saturday 14:00 EST; everything is due Sunday 11:00. src/rules/predict.py --level 3 scores 0.843 dev composite (signal 0.856, actionable 0.762, aspect F1 0.617, QWK 0.933, speaker 1.0, span 0.731, citation 1.0). Levels 2 and 3 were written after reading dev answers, so expect test to come in lower. No model has run, the Mac toolchain is untested and there are no teacher labels yet. Decisions in force (PROBLEM.md §0): at most 3B total parameters, Qwen3.5-2B first; frontier teacher labels on the 1,733 unlabeled items are allowed, never on test; rules keep the final say where they fire; self-consistency is rejected.

Where the dev points are

Each bar below replaces one part of the L3 output with the gold answer and reports the composite gain. These are ceilings, not forecasts. They show where to dig.

Other measured facts the ideas lean on:

  • Missed aspects: PubMed efficacy 10 (mostly +1), PubMed safety 6 (mostly −1), press efficacy 3, press safety 2, press convenience 2, news efficacy 2, the rest single items. Most have no cue word the rules know.
  • Signal errors (8): 4 are PubMed items gold calls none that L3 calls efficacy: a case report on another condition, a tumour-board case, a prognostic model and a screen for another drug.
  • Actionable errors (5): 4 are missed "yes": VTE prevention with amivantamab, dermatologic management with amivantamab-lazertinib, a real-world frontline study and ADAURA 8-year OS. 1 false "yes".
  • Span shape: matched PubMed spans score 0.638 against fda_event's 0.811. On the 46 gold aspects in PubMed, news, press and fda_label, the whole containing sentence scores 0.395 span F1, the best clause 0.625, and the best clause cut to 14 words 0.673. Gold press-release spans sit in 76–123-word "sentences" because the headline and navigation text has no full stops.
  • The scorer and extra aspects: score.py scores polarity, speaker and span only where gold and prediction both have the aspect (lines 88–96). An aspect gold leaves empty costs only aspect-F1 precision. The lesson doc's line "a wrong extra aspect can hurt up to four parts" holds only for a matched aspect with the wrong polarity or span.
  • Test is not shaped like dev: test has 30 news items against dev's 5, 10 fda_label against 2, and 1 fda_notice against none. 45 of 60 dev labels were drafted by a frontier model under the guide, then corrected by reviewer rulings.

Past learnings

docs/solutions/best-practices/composite-scorer-qwk-cliff-and-matched-only-metrics.md: the QWK cliff (1.0 with no variation, 0 after one disagreement), never submit zero aspects (0.167), and macro-F1 averages only classes present in gold, so each rare class is worth about 1/63 of the composite. Its "favour precision" advice is right for polarity and spans on matched aspects, but too strict for deciding whether to emit an aspect at all (idea 2).

Already looked up this run (web search only, about 11 searches, value low to moderate):

  • A small-model extraction benchmark puts Qwen3.5-2B level with a 2B Granite. IFEval figures disagree across sources.
  • Small Qwen3.5 models default to thinking off, but one report says some llama.cpp builds ignore the flag.
  • Fine-tuned encoders usually beat small LLMs at classification with a few thousand labels.
  • Picking a span by ID can't produce a non-verbatim quote.
  • Constrained yes/no token probabilities are better calibrated than asking the model for a confidence.
  • Retrieved few-shot examples beat fixed ones, though the evidence for small models is thin.
  • The web search found nothing confirming Qwen3.5 support in mlx-lm or llama.cpp. A follow-up look at the source code did: mlx-lm has mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5.py (it loads the text model and drops the vision weights), llama.cpp lists a qwen35 architecture, and ready GGUFs exist (unsloth/Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF).

More benchmark reading won't settle the model choice; a run on the Mac will.

Not worth researching: anything about test items (ground rules), the full articles behind news snippets (gold spans come from the snippet), and medical facts (the guide says to label what the text says).

I1

1. Run the teacher first: score it on dev, then build a silver scoreboard

Axis A · ScoreboardConfidence 85%Complexity Low–Medium (~2 h plus the API run)
Description
Make the teacher run the first thing that happens, not milestone M4 at 21:00.
  • Prompt the frontier teacher with the full guide and rulings plus same-source dev examples (never the item's own label). Run it on the 60 dev items and log its composite as a row. That number is the realistic ceiling for anything distilled from it, and a go/no-go for the model path.
  • Dedupe unlabeled against test (9 fda_event texts appear in both), then label the rest.
  • Hold out 300–400 silver items sampled to the test source mix.
  • One script scores any predictor on dev, the 15 human-reviewed items and the silver hold-out, prints the change with a bootstrap range, and appends the log row.
  • First use: run L2 and L3 on silver and see which dev-fitted rules hold up.
Basis
direct: README: "On the rest, a frontier model drafted the labels and we applied the reviewers' rulings" (45 of 60). The log header gives dev's 95% range as ±0.055, while every lever in this doc is worth +0.01 to +0.03, so dev alone can't rank them. PROBLEM §4.3: "Calibrate on dev first." DATA §8: 9 test fda_event texts also appear in unlabeled. Log E006 calls level 2 the "biggest single gain. Dev-fitted."
Rationale
Every other idea here needs these labels. The gate threshold (2), span tuning (3), the actionable and rare-class rules (5, 6) and any LoRA data (4) all read silver. If the teacher itself only gets about 0.88 on dev, a 2B copying it has little room over 0.843, and the hours should go to spans, item calls and the demo instead.
Downsides
Silver agrees with the drafter's habits, which is mostly what gold is too, so silver gains will overstate true quality. Use it to rank changes, not as truth. It costs API spend and a careful prompt (T3 can run it with North reviewing). It is already in the plan as M4. The change is to make it the first step and to use the dev score as a gate.
I2

2. Point the 2B at clauses, and add aspects above about 60% precision

Axis B · Aspect recallConfidence 55%Complexity Medium (~4–6 h)
Description
The model's job is to find the aspects the rules miss on PubMed, news and press items.
  • Candidates: the normalizer splits each item into sentences, then clauses (on ;, :, commas and "but / while / whereas"). Press releases split by headline and first paragraphs. Code numbers the clauses.
  • Model output: under a JSON grammar, the 2B returns {candidate_id, aspect, polarity} entries or nothing. It never writes span text, so code quotes the clause verbatim and citation validity stays at 1.0.
  • Gate: the probability of the constrained choice is the gate score. Emit an aspect when its estimated precision on silver is above about 0.6, and tune the exact cut on composite.
  • Prompt: the guide section for that source. Once the toolchain works, add 2–3 retrieved same-source silver examples.
  • Fallback: where the model returns nothing parseable, keep L3's output.
This also answers PROBLEM §13 Q1 (how strict the gate should be) and Q2 (point at clauses, not sentences).
Basis
direct: On dev, the whole containing sentence scores 0.395 span F1 against gold and the best clause cut to 14 words scores 0.673 (46 gold aspects, measured this run). The pointing unit roughly decides whether added aspects help or hurt.

Rerun of the break-even with those clause spans: a model that recovers half the 31 missed aspects gains +0.030 at precision 1.0, +0.025 at 0.8, +0.017 at 0.6, +0.012 at 0.5, +0.006 at 0.4 and −0.004 at 0.3, with 80% of polarities exact.

Why it's that low: score.py lines 88–96 only score polarity, speaker and span on matched aspects, so an extra aspect where gold is empty costs aspect-F1 precision and nothing else.

external: the web research found that ID-based selection can't produce non-verbatim spans, and that constrained-token probabilities are better calibrated than a confidence the model states (thin evidence).

Rationale
L3 runs at precision 1.0 with recall 0.45, which is stricter than the composite rewards. Recall matters about as much as precision here: at 0.8 precision, going from a quarter to half of the missed aspects takes the gain from +0.014 to +0.025. Turning the 2B's task into a choice among short, gold-shaped options plays to what small models do reliably.
Downsides
The realistic gain is about +0.01 to +0.025 on dev, close to the noise floor, so it can only be judged on silver (I1). The 0.673 figure assumes the model picks the best clause. Picking a neighbouring clause scores lower. Abstracts have odd punctuation, and a gold phrase that straddles two clauses only matches in part. It depends on I4 passing. The lesson doc's "favour precision" line should be corrected so nobody re-tightens the gate.
I3

3. Fit the span trimmer to silver spans, and split press releases properly

Axis C · Span shapeConfidence 65%Complexity Low–Medium (~2–3 h)
Description
Choose the trimming rule against hundreds of teacher spans instead of 13 dev ones, then confirm on dev. The rule covers which boundaries cut a clause, the maximum length, and whether to keep subject, verb and cue. Apply it to the rule cues (whose spans are often 2–4 words against gold's 7–12) and to I2's candidates. Separately, split press releases into headline, subheads and bullets, so a "sentence" is no longer 100 words of navigation text.
Basis
direct: An oracle span on the 25 aspects L3 already finds is worth +0.038, the largest single lever after missed aspects. Matched PubMed spans score 0.638 against fda_event's 0.811: L3's "manageable safety" (2 words) and "retrospectively analyzed" (2 words) are shorter than gold. Gold press-release spans sit in 76–123-word stretches without full stops (NW-0007, NW-0031, NW-0046). Log E008 widened spans to 16 words and span F1 fell from 0.731 to 0.629, so the length has to be fitted, not guessed.
Rationale
It needs no model and no Mac, so North can do it while the toolchain check and teacher run are going. It improves the rules-only submission (the safety net) and I2's candidates at the same time.
Downsides
Teacher spans may be a little longer or shorter than the drafter's, so check the human-reviewed 15 too. Expect +0.005 to +0.015, invisible on dev alone. Press releases are only 18 test items.
I4

4. Check the Mac toolchain (20–30 minutes); LoRA only overnight

Axis E · Model pathConfidence 85%Complexity Low (20–30 min, then overnight)
Description
Before anyone writes model code, check on the Mac that:
  • mlx-lm and llama.cpp both load Qwen3.5-2B;
  • decoding under a JSON schema or grammar works;
  • thinking stays off (check the chat template, not just the flag);
  • a 10-step LoRA runs (mlx-lm's default already covers every linear layer in a block, including the hybrid layers' in_proj_* and out_proj);
  • and record tokens per second.
Pin the versions that work. If either runtime fails, switch to the fallback the plan already names (Qwen2.5-3B or Llama-3.2-3B). LoRA on silver runs only overnight behind the G2 gate, and never blocks the daytime path. This answers §13 Q3. The copy-paste runbook for whoever has the Mac is drug track/MAC_CHECK.md.
Basis
external: The web search couldn't confirm support, but the source code does. mlx-lm has mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5.py with the Gated DeltaNet layers. llama.cpp's architecture list includes qwen35. unsloth/Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF has Q4 to Q8 files. The model is published as image+text, and mlx-lm loads only the text part.

One report says enable_thinking:false is ignored in some llama.cpp builds. The community warning that q/k/v/o-only LoRA misses the hybrid layers applies to other trainers: mlx-lm's LoRA defaults to every linear layer in a block. No source gives a clean "LoRA pays off after N examples" figure.

direct: PROBLEM §4.2 already names the fallback models and §10 the G2 gate.

Rationale
Support exists in the source code, but versions, JSON decoding and speed on this particular Mac are still unknown. Speed decides I2's cost per item and whether labeling about 2,000 items overnight fits. The check costs 20–30 minutes of someone else's time. Keeping LoRA overnight puts the uncertain bet on idle Mac time instead of daytime hours.
Downsides
If the fallback model is needed, prompt work starts on a different model. A model bake-off was considered and dropped: the evidence can't separate the candidates, and the fallbacks are already chosen.
I5

5. Fix the actionable misses and PubMed "none" items

Axis D · Item callsConfidence 55%Complexity Low–Medium (~2 h)
Description
Actionable: a prior by source and signal, plus a model "yes" on PubMed and news items that change practice for our own products: pivotal or long-term results, and studies on managing our drug's toxicities. PubMed "none": when the abstract's subject is another drug, a prognostic model or an unrelated condition, output signal none with no aspects. Add one question to the 2B's prompt: is osimertinib, amivantamab or lazertinib the subject? Check both fixes on silver before keeping them.
Basis
direct: On dev, 4 of 5 actionable errors are missed "yes" (PM-0005 VTE management with amivantamab, PM-0050 dermatologic management, PM-0022 real-world frontline, NW-0057 ADAURA 8-year OS). An oracle actionable is worth +0.034, the largest item-level lever. 4 of 8 signal errors are PubMed items gold calls none (PM-0028, PM-0035, PM-0064, PM-0090). An oracle signal is worth +0.020. Actionable F1 counts only the "yes" class, so each recovered "yes" counts heavily.
Rationale
Both are judgement calls the rules can't make from titles, and both fit naturally as extra fields in I2's single call. Together they're worth more than any one rare-class fix.
Downsides
Rules fitted to four dev items overfit easily. PM-0005 and PM-0050 have ordinary-looking titles, so a title rule won't find them. A partial fix is worth about +0.01, inside dev noise, so only silver can confirm it.
I6

6. Build the knowledge pack for rare signal classes in news and press

Axis D · Item callsConfidence 50%Complexity Medium (~3 h, T3)
Description
Use Tavily and Exa at build time to collect the lists that L2's title cues are missing:
  • rival EGFR and NSCLC drugs and their sponsors;
  • HTA and payer bodies by country (NICE, HAS, CADTH, PBS, G-BA, IQWiG and others);
  • guideline bodies;
  • trial name → product.
Feed them to rules for competitor_move (a rival leads the headline), access_pricing (a payer or HTA body acts), guideline and regulatory. Save every result to disk and log it in the ledger. No item text goes into a query.
Basis
direct: Test has 30 news items to dev's 5. DATA §3.6 expects competitor_move and access_pricing there, and dev has one example of each. Macro-F1 averages over the classes present in gold, so each rare class is about 1/9 of signal F1, or 1/63 of the composite. RESEARCH_README §1 already budgets this knowledge pack.
Rationale
This is the research that moves the leaderboard, not just the bonus. The same lists also group cards by event and supply cited facts for the pitch.
Downsides
The gain depends on the rare classes appearing in test and on titles carrying the cue, and dev can't show either. Check on silver. It takes T3's time from the pitch. Cue lists can misfire on press releases that name rivals in passing (log E007 saw the same with aspect cues).
