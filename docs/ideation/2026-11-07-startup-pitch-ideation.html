Ideation: a startup built on the drug-signal model, Tavily and Exa

Date: 2026-11-07 Topic: startup-pitch Focus: a startup to present that uses the local drug model together with Tavily and Exa Mode: elsewhere-software (the product and company, grounded in this repo)
Recommendation

Pitch idea 1 as the company and idea 3 as the way in. Tavily and Exa watch the public conversation, and the local model finds the aspects where public evidence is split or thin. Those gaps become the questions for the next advisory board. The board's private transcript is then labeled offline, in the same schema and by the same model, and the product shows where what experts say in private differs from what the public record says.

This is the one framing where both halves are needed. Tavily and Exa only help with public data, and a local model only matters for private data. It also matches the track's name ("Private Signals") and the sponsor's world: Within3 runs advisory boards and HCP insights (check the wording on their site before it goes on a slide).

A rules-first labeler (0.843 dev composite) that tags each item with a signal type, whether it's actionable, and five aspects (efficacy, safety, convenience, access/cost, evidence quality). Each aspect gets a polarity from −2 to +2, a speaker role and a verbatim quote. Qwen3.5-2B is the planned model path, built for transcripts first. Citations are exact substrings by construction (PROBLEM.md §0, lean plan).
  • How it's judged beyond the leaderboard. A network-off run on private transcripts, citations that support claims, how the trends look, usefulness to a launch expert, cost per 100 items, and bonus points for effective Tavily/Exa use and a credible startup (PROBLEM.md §2).

It has three tiers (Public Pulse, Private Signals, Launch Room), a per-brand annual licence, and a moat of ontology, per-TA fine-tunes and provenance (PROBLEM.md §7). It's a solid baseline, but Tavily and Exa only appear in it as a "live monitoring" tier.

1. Public questions, private answers

I1Axis: data sideConfidence 75%Complexity: Medium
Description
Each week, Tavily (dated news, regulator and HTA pages) and Exa (semantic search, find-similar for papers and commentary) pull the public conversation about a brand. The local model labels it. The product then picks the aspects where public evidence is contested (polarity split across speakers, for example HCPs negative on safety while the company is positive) or thin (few items, low evidence quality). It turns those into a short question set for the next advisory board or MSL round, with the public quotes that prompted each question attached. The answers come back as a private transcript, which the same model labels offline in the same schema. The output is a gap map: per aspect and speaker, what the public record says next to what your experts said in the room, each side backed by quotes.
Basis
direct: the track is "Private Signals", and judges run the model network-off on private transcripts we haven't seen (PROBLEM.md §1–2). The architecture already splits network-on build-time research from network-off inference (PROBLEM.md §4, §6). The aspect/polarity/speaker schema is source-agnostic, so a transcript and a PubMed abstract produce comparable rows. The transcript path with speaker roles is lean plan item 5. reasoned: the hardest pitch question is "why do you need web search and a local model?" In this design the answer is structural, not bolted on: Tavily and Exa only help on public data, and a local model only matters on private data.
Rationale
It turns the two judged extras (the Tavily/Exa bonus and the network-off transcript run) into one product story instead of two separate demo beats. It also gives the sponsor an obvious "this plugs into what you already do" reading, since advisory boards are where private expert opinion lives. The gap ("experts in the room are more worried about rash than the press coverage suggests") is the kind of insight a launch team would act on, which is the usefulness criterion.
Downsides
Question generation and the contested-aspect detector are new work. For the demo they can be simple rules over the labeled corpus (an aspect where speakers' mean polarity differs by 2+ points or there are fewer than N items). The gap map needs a real private transcript. Mock ones (invented people) are already planned, but the gap will be staged, so say so. There's a risk of looking like we're pitching Within3's own product back to them, so frame it as a layer on top of an insights platform, not a replacement.

2. Brand-plan claim watch

I2Axis: output and workflowConfidence 65%Complexity: Medium
Description
A launch team doesn't read feeds. It defends a plan built on a handful of claims, for example "subcutaneous dosing removes the infusion-chair burden" or "the overall survival benefit outweighs the added toxicity". The team enters 5–10 claims, each mapped to a product, an aspect and a direction. Every week, Tavily and Exa pull evidence that bears on each claim, the local model labels it, and each claim gets a scorecard: supporting vs contradicting quotes, the trend, and who is saying it. An alert fires when a claim's balance flips. Each actionable signal is routed to the function that owns the claim (medical, market access, PV).
Basis
direct: the five aspects map closely onto brand-plan pillars (efficacy, safety, convenience, access/cost, evidence quality), and the problem statement's second weekly question is "how is the conversation moving?" (PROBLEM.md §1). Exa's semantic search takes a natural-language description as the query (RESEARCH_README.md §3, "pages like this one"), which suits claim-shaped queries. reasoned: grouping signals by the claim they threaten makes "actionable" concrete. The card says what it's actionable for.
Rationale
It's the most "would a launch expert act on it?" shaped output, and it gives Tavily and Exa a job they're actually good at (targeted retrieval against a stated hypothesis) instead of generic monitoring. It also demos well: one claim card turning amber with the quote that did it.
Downsides
Brand claims are confidential, and sending them to a search API as queries leaks strategy. Queries must be generic public terms derived locally (drug, outcome, setting), never the claim's wording. That's the same R1 discipline we already follow, and worth saying out loud in the pitch. Claim-to-evidence matching beyond the aspect, product and polarity filter is unbuilt, but the filter is enough for v1.

3. Listen without the AE fear (the way in)

I3Axis: buyer and jobConfidence 60%Complexity: Low
Description
Lead the go-to-market with pharmacovigilance, not insight. Every item, transcript turn or field note that carries non-neutral safety gets the ICH E2D four-box check (AE quote, product, patient cue, reporter role), with each box pointing at its quote. The result is a triage queue for the safety team. Each decision is stored with the quote, source URL, retrieval time (from the Tavily/Exa provenance log), model version and reviewer verdict, so the record doubles as an inspection-ready audit trail. Once PV trusts it, the insight product (ideas 1 and 2) sells into the same account.
Basis
direct: report D fact 1 (E2D(R1) names MAH digital listening as a source with reporting duties; the clock starts when a reviewer identifies the four minimum criteria), fact 2 (Pierce 2017: 935,246 posts became 13 causality reviews after three months of manual curation) and fact 5 (65% of the top 20 pharmas banned ChatGPT). The AE triage flag is already lean plan item 6, and citations are exact substrings by construction. reasoned: safety spend is mandatory and insight spend is discretionary. Fear of triggering AE reporting duties is also a reason teams avoid listening to private channels at all, so removing it unlocks idea 1.
Rationale
It gives the pitch a buyer with a regulatory reason to pay this year, and it's the cheapest idea to demo because the flag is already planned. The audit-trail angle makes "no quote, no claim" a compliance feature, not just a scoring trick.
Downsides
PV buyers are conservative, and validated (GxP) software is a heavy lift. A missed AE is far worse than a missed insight, and we have no recall numbers for the 2B on AE detection. Tavily and Exa play a smaller role here (public forums and news only), so it can't be the whole story for the bonus. Pitch it as the way in, with idea 1 as the company.

4. A new launch in an afternoon

I4Axis: scale and moatConfidence 60%Complexity: Medium
Description
Type a drug name and indication. Exa (structured agent runs, or MCP search) and Tavily (dated news, regulator and HTA pages) build that launch's knowledge pack: aliases, trial-to-NCT map, rivals, regulator, HTA and payer bodies with speaker roles, and a dated event timeline. Every entry keeps its source URL. The local pipeline then labels the public backlog, and the dashboard is live for a new brand the same day. Tavily and Exa are the onboarding engine. The customer's private data still never leaves.
Basis
direct: this knowledge pack is already the "Must" Tavily/Exa job in PROBLEM.md §6 (trials, rivals, bodies, timeline, built by an Exa Agent with outputSchema plus Tavily search), and "per-therapeutic-area fine-tunes" is the stated moat (§7). reasoned: the obvious judge objection is "you built this for two drugs". Showing that the per-launch setup is a search job rather than a consulting project answers it, and a per-brand licence only scales if onboarding is cheap.
Rationale
It's the strongest "effective use of Tavily and Exa" story, because they become the reason the business scales, not a demo garnish. It reuses work we'd do anyway for the two track drugs.
Downsides
Rules are tuned to EGFR NSCLC sources, so a new therapeutic area would lean on the model and quality is unmeasured. There's no Exa API key, so agent runs go through MCP or Tavily instead. A live third-drug run on stage could go wrong, so prerecord it or show the generated pack only.

5. Launch analog curves

I5Axis: what Tavily and Exa doConfidence 50%Complexity: Medium
Description
"Is this normal?" is the question a trend line can't answer alone. Exa's find-similar and Tavily's date-bounded search retrieve coverage of past comparable launches (earlier targeted therapies in the same setting, for example), and the local model labels it in the same schema. Each aspect's trend is then drawn against a band of where analog launches sat at the same week. A drop outside the band is the signal, not a drop on its own.
Basis
direct: the sentiment trend report (−2 to +2 by product × aspect × speaker, week over week) is a required deliverable and a P0 demo screen (PROBLEM.md §1, §8). Exa find-similar is already slotted for "expand this signal" (§6). reasoned: raw sentiment lines are hard to read without a baseline. Every new launch adds to the analog library, so the baseline compounds into a dataset competitors don't have.
Rationale
It's a strong single visual for the trends criterion, and a compounding-data moat that's easy to explain in one sentence.
Downsides
Historical coverage from search is uneven (older news snippets and links rot), so the bands could be noisy. Choosing which launches count as analogs is a judgment call that needs a domain expert. It's not buildable to a credible standard by tomorrow, so pitch it as roadmap with this mock-up.

6. Live advisory-board copilot

I6Axis: output and workflowConfidence 45%Complexity: High
Description
Idea 1, in real time. During a virtual advisory board, the local model labels each turn as it's spoken. The moderator's side panel fills an aspect × speaker grid as the meeting goes, raises the AE triage flag the moment an HCP describes a patient's side effect, and surfaces "public evidence says otherwise" cards from the locally cached Tavily/Exa pack. The moderator can probe a point while the experts are still in the room.
Basis
direct: the transcript path (lean plan item 5), AE flag (item 6) and offline knowledge pack (PROBLEM.md §6) are the three parts it needs. Demo step 4 already pastes a mock transcript into the Analyze screen (§8). reasoned: post-meeting summaries arrive after the chance to follow up is gone, and a model on the moderator's laptop needs no IT approval to sit in a confidential meeting.
Rationale
It's the biggest "wow" version of the air-gap demo, and it ties the product to the sponsor's live-meeting setting.
Downsides
It depends on per-turn latency on the Mac, which isn't measured yet (lean plan item 8). Real-time UX is a different product from a weekly brief. It overlaps idea 1, so present it as idea 1's live mode, not a separate company. If latency is poor, show it as a replay of a timed transcript.
How they fit in a 25-second close

Company: idea 1 (public questions, private answers). Way in: idea 3 (AE-safe listening for PV). Why it scales: idea 4 (a new launch in an afternoon, powered by Tavily and Exa). Ideas 2, 5 and 6 are features and roadmap lines for the slides. The existing tiers in PROBLEM.md §7 still work underneath: Public Pulse is the Tavily/Exa half, and Private Signals is the offline half.
